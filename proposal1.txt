My main extracurricular at the moment is the algorithmic portfolio of the PPLE investment club. 

  Aside from traditional quant methods, I'm wondering whether sentiment analysis can be used to enhance
signals for given stocks, and so an investigation into the correlation between an uptick/downturn in a 
given stock price and the frequency of positive/negative mentions of the stock (checking sentiment from a list of certain keywords)


Research Question: How strong is the correlation between discussion of stocks in the media and price changes? or To what extent is media sentiment predictive of/correlated to stock price in the short term?

This project will be done using the Alpacas API, an industry standard paper-trading and fintech service, to gather both price and news historical data. Initially, only the volume of mentions of a given ticker in headlines on a given day will be used, and compared with the % price change over the following 7 days. In a potentially more advanced version, basic sentiment analysis will be performed on these headlines, scoring a ticker positively or negatively, and then comparing this result with the % price change over the following 7 days.

To establish correlation, tests such as Pearson's R or Spearman's Rho will be performed upon the data.

The final product will likely be graphical, displaying multiple stocks' price graphs with news mentions overlaid (positive or negative), as well as tables/graphs demonstrating correlation in raw data, as well as examples of strong correlation


Skills to be used: APIs (alpacas), file-handling (recording test data for comparison), sentiment analysis (likely basic, using lists of certain positively/negatively correlated keywords), graphical representation (creating graphs etc.)
